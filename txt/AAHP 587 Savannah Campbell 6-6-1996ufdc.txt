AAHP 587 Savannah Campbell 6-6-1996 Interviewed by Vivian Hill on June 6, 1996 63 minutes | 47 pages



Abstract: In this interview, Savannah Campbell recalls her childhood in Twin Lakes, Florida, where she was raised on a farm. She remembers that her family survived by growing, eating, and selling crops such as string beans, tomatoes, sugarcane, and potatoes. Campbell recalls that she attended a nearby elementary school until the fourth grade, when members of the Ku Klux Klan burned down her school and several other buildings in the community. Campbell worked as a custodian at Buckingham Army Base before beginning a teacher career at Sunnyland. Campbell endured several major hardships over the course of her life, including the loss of both of her children and her first husband. In spite of this, Campbell maintains a positive outlook on life. She explains that she is spending her retirement as the mother of her church, Saint Paul’s, where she assists the pastor and acts as church treasurer.



Keywords: [Fort Meyers, Florida, Black community, family history, Twin Lakes, army, church, racial terrorism, Ku Klux Klan]
