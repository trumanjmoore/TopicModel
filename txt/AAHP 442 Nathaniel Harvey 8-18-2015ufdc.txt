AAHP 442 Nathaniel Harvey African American History Project (AAHP)

Interviewed by Vickie Oldham on August 18, 2015 36 minutes | 25 pages



Abstract: In his interview, Nathan Harvey describes living and working in Johnson Camp, in Sarasota. He mentions a little church there, which also served as a place for education, and recalls some of his past teachers. Harvey also recollects what it was like to live in Newtown and Ontario, and race relations during those times. He mentions some advice for town leaders and the community to improve in terms of racial tensions, and family life in Newtown.



Keywords: [African American History; Newtown; Sarasota; Farming]

	O:	I can imagine it being very warm if you all had to do this in the summertime. H:	Most of it was it done in the summertime, both winter and summer.

I'm not saying we didn't have some of it but not like it is now. On and on it was pretty nice.

	O:	Really?

If we had something to say about one another, we went in our bedroom, we shut the door. I never hit her in her life, she never hit me. So we got along good. We taught our kids to do unto others as you wish them to do unto you. Just be nice. That don't mean you got to let nobody run over you. Just be nice and you'll get along, and they did.

started with just Adam and Eve. And there was no color then. And we all started from that. And so therefore when we die, we all trying to get to heaven and when we get to heaven we not gonna get to heaven as no Black and White. When we get to heaven, it's gonna be no, "Okay say the Blacks over here, White ones go over here." So if we separate down here on earth, you think we gonna be separated in heaven? So if we separate down here and we can't love each other down here–you gonna love me in heaven but you can't love me down here? Come on, think about it. Just think about that. You got to love me down here first before you get to heaven. If you can't speak to me down here, you think you gonna speak to me in heaven?
