AAHP 187 Melverine Morris African American History Project (AAHP)

Interview conducted by Maria Muñoz on September 2, 2011 1 minute | 20 pages



Abstract: This interview was conducted at an event celebrating the legacy of Robert Jenkins Ellerson High School in Starke, Florida. Melverine Morris attended during integration, so she experienced the transition to Bradford High School. The interview is very brief.





Keywords: [African American History; Bradford County, Florida; RJE High School]





























































For information on terms of use of this interview, please see the SPOHP Creative Commons license at http://ufdc.ufl.edu/AfricanAmericanOralHistory.



AAHP 187

Interviewee: Melverine Morris Interviewer: Maria Muñoz Date: September 2, 2011



	Mu:	Okay, so what is your name? Mo:	My name is Melverine Morris. Mu:	And are you a graduate of RJE?

	Mo:	Actually, no. The school was being phased out by 1969. So I moved from here to Bradford High, and I graduated there in 1970—[19]69-70.

	Mu:	Okay. And do you still live in Starke, Florida? Mo:	Yes. I live here.

	Mu:	And what does today’s event mean to you today?



	Mo:	It means a lot to me, because I come back to be able to see that the school is still here, and I hadn’t been able to come to the events up until this point. And I was glad because I had come back from Gainesville, back to here, about three years ago, and this is actually my first event. And I’m excited about it.

	Mu:	Great. Is there anything else that you’d like to add, that we should know about today’s event?

	Mo:	Well this is the history, it’s a part of our history in this area, and I’m glad it’s still standing. And actually, they’re using the gym, they’re using all the other facilities, as best they can to get educational events. You know, continuing educational events.

	Mu:	Great! Thank you. [End of interview]
