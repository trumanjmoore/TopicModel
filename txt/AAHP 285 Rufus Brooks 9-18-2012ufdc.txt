AAHP 285 Rufus Brooks

African American History Program (AAHP) Interviewed by Gigi Simmons in on September 18, 2012

1 hour, 6 minutes | 43 pages





Abstract: Rufus “Nine” Brooks is interviewed in his home in Gainesville, Florida. He begins the interview discussing his childhood and fathers’ career as a carpenter; his family lived on a quarter and did work in the Atkins Crate Mill in Otter Creek, and then later Gainesville. He then talks about different jobs he held throughout his life, such as working as a bell hop and factory labor, and mentions what his siblings did and how it was affected by their gender and skin color. Mr. Brooks discusses life without shoes, electricity, plumbing, and medicine. He talks about how he avoided going into military service and what daily life was like during times of racial segregation. Mr. Brooks starts to end the interview with a short descriptions of what he remembers when President Kennedy and Martin Luther King Jr. were both assassinated and finishes with a message to all: the truth will set you free.
