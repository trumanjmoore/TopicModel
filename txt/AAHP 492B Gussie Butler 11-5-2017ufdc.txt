AAHP 492B Gussie Butler African American History Project (AAHP)

Interviewed by Fletcher Crow and Steven Butler on November 5, 2017 1 hour, 26 minutes | 55 pages



Abstract: Gussie Butler is a Gainesville resident, and in this interview, he talks about how East Gainesville used to be. At the time he lived on Main Street closest to the Mount Pleasant Community and he talks about the changes to the community and Black businesses that were essential as well. He references places such as Lincoln High, Blue Flame, Wabash Hall, and the Cotton Club, and their importance to the African American community. Butler doesn’t only mention these locations, but rather he goes into detail about what each place meant to him regarding his own experiences. He also talks about the churches that were staples to the Black community in Gainesville as well as how his life intersects with this town. He started out as a janitor for Alachua General and then owned a barbershop on Seminary Lane.





Keywords: [East Gainesville, Fifth Avenue, 5th Avenue, African American History Project, Met’s Theater, Mama Lo’s Restaurant, Dr. Debose Dental Office, Sander’s Cleaners, Heritage Trail; Seminary Lane; Mount Pleasant Community; Black businesses; Alachua General Hospital; Gainesville, Florida; ]
