AAHP 130 Jerome Josey

African American History Project (AAHP) Interview conducted by Ryan Morini on September 3, 2010

7 minutes | 5 pages



Abstract: This interview was conducted at the 2010 Black Alumni Weekend at the University of Florida. Jerome Josey relates why he came to UF, some of his experiences while a student there, and what he hopes to get out of membership in the Association of Black Alumni.





Keywords: [African American History; University of Florida]



























































For information on terms of use of this interview, please see the SPOHP Creative Commons license at http://ufdc.ufl.edu/AfricanAmericanOralHistory.



AAHP 130

Interviewee: Jerome Josey Interviewer: Ryan Morini Date: September 9, 2010



	M:	This is Ryan Morini and I’m interviewing Jerome Josey and we’re here at the UF Association of Black Alumni Weekend. When were you born?

	J:	January 20, [19]64.



	M:	Where were you born? J:	Delray Beach, Florida.

	M:	Did you grow up around there? J:	Uh-huh.

	M:	What did your parents do for a living?



	J:	My father was an engineer and my mother was a nurse. M:	Where did you go to school, growing up?

	J:	In Palm Beach County, Atlantic High School. M:	Did you always plan on going to college?

	J:	Sure.



	M:	Just always knew?



	J:	My parents decided that for me.



	M:	I see. What years did you attend UF? J:	1981-[19]85.

	M:	How did you make that decision?



	J:	Just some classmates of mine, we were talking about where we wanted to attend college and I chose UF.

	M:	That was a definite decision? You didn’t have alternates? J:	I had alternates, but I chose UF.





	M:	Where else did you choose between? J:	Morehouse College in Atlanta.

	M:	What were your first impressions of Gainesville? What did you think when you got here?

	J:	It was kind of rural.



	M:	Fair enough, kind of a small town. What about the campus itself? J:	The campus was wonderful, great.

	M:	Where did you live while you were here?



	J:	I lived in the Towers, Jennings, Broward, and then I moved off campus. M:	Where did you move to?

	J:	Some apartments off, I forget what street it was. It’s been like thirty years. M:	I understand. How did you pay for school?

	J:	My parents.



	M:	They saved up, that’s a good thing. Where did you get your food? Where did you go to eat?

	J:	In the cafeteria.



	M:	What about if you were going out or something to have fun or to eat?



	J:	Just places around campus. I remember there was a Leonardo’s Pizza and some chicken place that was near there. We ate there a lot.

	M:	Pretty much close to campus. Where did you get your hair cut if I might ask? J:	They used to have a University Barbershop over at the Reitz Union.

	M:	Just right in the Reitz. When you were here did you have a particular mentor or anyone you would go to?







J:

No.

M:

No particular people?

J:

No.

M:

What did you major in?

J:

Criminal Justice.

M:

Were you certain that was your major the whole time you were here?

J:

Pretty much.

M:

Did you know that before you even got here?

J:

Not really. I took a Criminal Justice course to satisfy a social science credit and I



enjoyed it.

M:

Fair enough. Were there a lot of other Black people in your major?

J:

No not a whole lot.

M:

Did that ever cause any tensions for you?

J:

It was fine.

M:

Fair enough. What did you do for fun?

J:

They used to have parties at the Union and frat parties.

M:

Did you go to any of the sports stuff, homecoming, Gator Growl?

J:

Yeah, I used to do all of that stuff.

M:

Did you go to church while you were here?

J:

I did.

M:

Where did you go to church?

J:

I think it was called Williams Temple Church of God in Christ.

M:

Where was that, roughly?







J:

A little east of town, down east of the school.

M:

Did you engage much with the local Black community or you were mostly on



campus?

J:

Mostly on campus.

M:

Did you ever go home on weekends?

J:

Yeah.

M:

How often did you do that?

J:

Once every couple of months.

M:

Just to stay in touch. What’s it like to come back now?

J:

It’s fun to come back now to games.

M:

Yeah it’s always fun. Has campus changed a lot?

J:

It has.

M:

How so?

J:

A lot more buildings that weren’t here twenty-five years ago.

M:

What’s it like to come to this event and meet graduates from different years?

J:

Networking; you meet a lot of different people, see people you went to school



with.

M:

Do you have any regrets? Do you have good memories about your time here?

J:

Good memories.

M:

Anything to elaborate on?

J:

I enjoyed my time here. It was probably the best couple years of my life, you



know, college.

M:

Fair enough. Do you have any parting thoughts or final words for posterity?





	J:	I mean it’s a great institution. I’m glad that I chose it. I come back a couple times a year to the games. I live in Georgia, so it’s fun being up there during football season.

	M:	I can imagine. Well thank you very much. [End of Interview]
