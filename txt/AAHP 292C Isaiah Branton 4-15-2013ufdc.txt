	D:	Good afternoon—morning? This is Justin Dunnavant here with Mr. Isaiah Branton. Today is April 15th and we are in the Oral History Program right now going through some more of Mr. Branton’s photographs. Right now we are on photograph number 086 and can you tell me a little bit about this one?

	B:	Okay this photo is my father Reverend Jackson Branton. The picture was taken after he had been to a funeral and they told him to put the robe back on so they can take his picture again. He definitely didn’t want to take it because they had rain coats on the thing because he had it all ironed out but he thought he was through for the day and he wrapped it up. He put it back on and it had rain but he still did it with diligence to take the picture. He was a tall man, very firm; a very articulate preacher and could pray, he could pray and he was born in 1908 November 25th, on Thanksgiving Day. His mother was Arley Branton; his daddy was John Wesley Branton. His granddaddy was Jackson—Reverend Jackson Branton who was born in 1864. He was named after his granddaddy which was a pastor.

	D:	Where did he preach at?



	B:	Everywhere. He was a moderator. We preached in Georgia, all through Florida, Orlando but he had a church in Newberry, had one in Lake City, had one in Bronson, he had one in Chiefland, had one in Gainesville when they transferred pastors to different churches? He passed on in there and then he became a moderator where he put pastors in place and then he go around and examined them to see if they were you know on task so they worked him.





	D:	Yeah?



	B:	They worked him.



	D:	What denomination was he affiliated with?



	B:	Missionary Baptist and his grandfather established a bunch of churches during the time of the Bible movement and the Bible belt. He established a lot of Missionary Baptist Church all away from Monticello, Florida all the way to Mobile, to North Carolina. He rode a horse because he started passing in the 1800s.

	D:	Okay. Where you around when this photograph was taken? B:	Oh yeah, I was in the car.

	D:	Oh okay. You know where this was at or when it was taken?



B:  I think that photograph was taken in a place called Gulf Hammock. D:  Gulf Hammock.

B:  He had members out there. I’m sure that’s where it was taken after preaching and that was taken—I was a little boy during that time. I think that photograph was taken in lemme see, I would say around 1963.

D:  And he did funerals, did he do weddings as well? B:  Everything.

D:   Everything?



	B:	Everything. He was qualified, ordained to do all of that. He did everything. He preached—the saddest funeral he preached was one of my classmates, he was nine years old, nine or ten, another guy was playing with a gun and shot him and killed him, and that’s the saddest funeral I ever saw my dad preach. He was





about ten years old, and he got shot through the naked head and he died and dad preached that funeral. He did bacalars too you know.

	D:	Oh okay.



	B:	Yeah so. Let me tell you, preachers ain’t got what he had. He knew the Bible. He studied daily. I have never seen a day, unless he was sick, that he didn’t pick up the Bible for two or three hours. He didn’t want nobody to disturb him. Yeah so, that was a man of God there. I can truly say now.

	D:	Did you follow him around when he went on his different—?



	B:	Yeah and sometimes we had to stay with other church members because he would go into a week revival and he didn’t want to miss his school. So we had to stay with other people sometimes. But he was on the move and also, he was a supervisor on his job.

	D:	Oh okay. He worked in addition to being a preacher?



	B:	He worked for what you call, ghostly llama company but he was a supervisor. He also taking the men to work and got paid for doing that so he was making money then he had about a five or six acre farm; cows and hogs, grew produce. He was a workaholic. I couldn’t follow his footsteps. [Laughter] No way, he had big hands too.

	D:	Can you talk a little bit about the revivals?
